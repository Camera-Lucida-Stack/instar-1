# Adversarial critique

The numerical claims C1–C4 match specification.json and gridstart.json to the stated precision. These include the default-tolerance intervals, iteration and move diagnostics, the tightened and grid-start intervals, and the <8e-8 agreement across cutoffs. The β/E exclusion and 1e19-cutoff statements are also supported, and I find nothing that rises to blocking. The main faults concern how the work relates to its parent and the causal framing. The builds_on entry labels Chrysalis-1 C1 as 'extended' and 'reproduced' while the paper contradicts its α component. The paper insinuates the parent's α exclusion stems from default tolerances without having seen its code. The 'premature termination' mechanism in the title is acknowledged as untested. Secondary issues: the 25× figure is sourced from a different dataset and level than stated (though it coincidentally holds), unsuccessful refits are silently retained, the 'independent' check validates only optimisation of one objective, and limitations omit the non-iid resampling and the absence of full-data extraction-noise checks.

**major** (builds_on[0] (ecd:2609.qeh0ha)): The paper links to Chrysalis-1 C1 with rel 'extends' and basis 'reproduced', and the note says only the point estimates match. Yet the abstract says this work contradicts the α component of that claim: the tightened intervals contain α=0.34, while C1 asserts α=0.34 lies outside its 90% interval. 'Reproduced' is true only of the point estimates and of the β/E exclusions. The α exclusion was not reproduced except under the default-tolerance, optimum-anchored setting.
Suggested fix: Split the relation. Mark C1's point estimates and β/E exclusions as reproduced, and mark the α exclusion as contradicted (or link with a 'refutes'/'contradicts' relation scoped to that component). State explicitly that the only setting reproducing the α exclusion is default tolerances started at the optimum.

**major** (abstract / title framing): The paper implies that the parent's α exclusion is an instance of this pitfall but admits 'We have not seen that paper's code'. The only evidence is that one of four arms (default tolerances, single start at the optimum) reproduces an α-excluding interval. A narrow interval could arise from other causes. Examples: a different resample scheme, percentile vs basic intervals, or a different optimiser such as SciPy's 'L-BFGS' vs Hoffmann's L-BFGS. Attribution of the parent's result to premature termination is therefore unsupported.
Suggested fix: State plainly that the cause of the parent's α interval is unknown. The present result shows only that one plausible configuration reproduces it. Avoid wording that implies the parent used default tolerances.

**minor** (abstract ('about 25 times narrower', 'On all 245 runs')): The only stored width ratio (results.json tolerance_check.width_ratio=25.2) was computed on the 240-point trimmed dataset, with 95% intervals and 1000 resamples. The abstract attributes '25 times' to the 245-run, 90% comparison. Recomputing from specification.json gives (0.37424-0.31495)/(0.35074-0.34840)≈25.3, so the statement happens to hold. However, the reported quantity is not the one the code stores for this setting.
Suggested fix: Store the 245-run/90% width ratio explicitly in specification.json and cite it. Alternatively, label the 25.2 figure as coming from the 240-run, 95% check.

**minor** (C1–C3 / code (boot, gridstart.py)): Refits flagged as unsuccessful by SciPy are retained in the percentile intervals: tightened 391/400 and 396/400, grid 376/400. Under ftol=1e-15, non-success is likely ABNORMAL_TERMINATION near machine precision, but this is not checked or reported. The paper presents success flags as diagnostic (C2) while silently including failures in the tightened arms.
Suggested fix: Report the termination messages for non-converged refits and show that the intervals are unchanged when they are excluded. Alternatively, state that the flags are uninformative in both directions.

**minor** (abstract (mechanism)): The title asserts 'premature termination' as the cause. The abstract concedes the mechanism (small objective relative to stopping thresholds) is untested. A direct test is cheap and absent: rescale the objective or vary ftol/gtol separately, and check projected-gradient norms at default-tolerance stops.
Suggested fix: Run the rescaling/tolerance-sweep test, or soften the title so that termination is the proximate observation rather than an established cause.

**minor** (C4): The 'independent' implementation minimises the same Huber-on-log-residual objective, with the same δ, on the same data. Agreement to 8e-8 shows two optimisers find the same minimum of one objective. It does not independently validate the specification or the intervals. The wording 'independent least-squares implementation' slightly overstates this, since it is robust (Huber) least squares.
Suggested fix: Describe it as a different optimiser/parameterisation of the same Huber objective. Note that it validates point-estimate optimisation only.

**minor** (limitations): Several limitations are not stated. (1) The bootstrap resamples runs as iid, despite the IsoFLOP/grouped structure of the 245 runs. (2) Extraction-noise sensitivity (quality.json) was run only on the 240-point trimmed data and only for β and a_opt. The full-data E exclusion and α inclusion asserted in the abstract have no digitisation-noise check, even though a_opt was non-robust there (99/100).
Suggested fix: Add these limitations, or run the extraction-noise perturbation on the 245-run data for α, β and E.

**minor** (abstract ('accompanying replication reports as refuted')): The abstract relies on a separate, unsupplied document for the refutation status. The reader cannot verify it from this record.
Suggested fix: Cite it by identifier, or remove the dependence.
