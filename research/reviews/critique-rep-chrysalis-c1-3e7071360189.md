# Adversarial critique

Payload SHA-256: 3e7071360189e69f252a6331c6c692026f94742d0b36706ad087c705877ac2d2

All numerical values quoted in the evidence match specification.json and gridstart.json: the point estimates, the 90% intervals under the tightened, single-start, grid-start and default arms, the 391/396 convergence counts, the median of 12 iterations, and the 7.6e-8 independent-implementation agreement. The core finding is well supported: alpha=0.34 lies inside the 90% bootstrap interval under every tightened scheme, including an optimum-independent 243-start grid, while beta and E are excluded. The weaknesses lie in framing and completeness. The 'refuted' label rests on the compound structure of the parent claim and obscures that most of it replicates. Sameness of grid and objective with the parent is asserted without access to the parent's code, which matters for the proposed default-tolerance explanation. The grid-start convergence count (376/400) and far-optima count (157) are omitted. The success flags under ftol=1e-15 are of doubtful meaning. Extraction noise and the mismatched configuration of the quality checks are not stated as limitations. None of these issues makes the central alpha-inside-interval result false, so nothing is blocking.

**minor** (outcome): Labelling the replication 'refuted' is technically defensible only because the parent's C1 is a conjunction over alpha, beta and E. The point estimates and four of the five sub-assertions replicate (point estimates, beta excluded, E excluded). The bare outcome label invites readers to infer that the parent's headline coefficient mismatch failed to replicate, which is the opposite of what was found.
Suggested fix: Use a partial-replication outcome if the protocol allows one. Otherwise state explicitly in the opening sentence that the target claim is compound and that only its alpha component fails, and that beta and E exclusion replicate.

**minor** (evidence ("Same data..., objective... and grid of initialisations")): Two parts of the 'same' claim are not supported, since the authors admit they have not seen the parent's code. The parent's abstract says only 'L-BFGS from an init grid'. The paper nonetheless asserts the same grid, and the same objective 'summed', without evidence. Sum versus mean matters for the paper's own proposed explanation. SciPy's ftol stopping criterion depends on objective scale, so whether default-tolerance refits stall depends on this unverified choice.
Suggested fix: Say the grid follows Hoffmann et al.'s published grid rather than the parent's. State that summation is an assumption. Note that the default-tolerance explanation is conditional on the parent also summing, or show the default-tolerance result under a mean objective.

**minor** (evidence (convergence counts)): Three tightened schemes are described: five jittered starts, single start at the optimum, and the 243-point grid. Convergence is reported only for 'the two schemes' (391, 396). The grid-start run has the lowest success count, 376/400, and this is omitted. Also omitted is that 157/400 grid-start optima land more than 1.0 (log-parameter units) from the full-data optimum. The paper leans on the grid-start run as its independence check, so these diagnostics belong in the text.
Suggested fix: Report 376/400 converged and 157/400 far optima for the grid-start run. Add a sensitivity check restricting all three intervals to converged refits only.

**minor** (code (chinchilla.TIGHT)): ftol=1e-15 is at or below double-precision resolution for L-BFGS-B's relative reduction test. Reported 'success' flags under the tightened setting may therefore partly reflect abnormal line-search termination rather than meaningful convergence. Treating success counts as evidence of reliability is weaker than presented.
Suggested fix: Report the termination messages or status codes and the final projected-gradient norms for the tightened refits, rather than relying on r.success alone.

**minor** (evidence ("five starts per resample")): The five starts are fixed jitters of the full-data optimum (the JIT offsets). They are not independent initialisations, and the tightened arm is also described without the word 'jittered'. Only the grid-start arm is optimum-independent.
Suggested fix: Describe the five-start scheme as five jittered starts around the full-data optimum in both places.

**minor** (limitations): Two robustness gaps are not stated as limitations. First, digitisation or extraction error in the SVG-reconstructed data is not propagated into the alpha conclusion. Second, the quality checks (parameter recovery, extraction noise) were run on the 240-point trimmed set with 95% intervals, not on the 245-point, 90% configuration used here. The alpha conclusion is in the direction that extra noise would only strengthen, but the extraction-noise check covers only beta and a_opt.
Suggested fix: State that the quality checks were run on a different configuration. State that extraction noise is unmodelled for this replication, and note that it would widen rather than narrow the intervals.

**minor** (evidence (parent explanation)): The suggestion that the parent used a default-tolerance bootstrap started at the optimum is speculation. It is supported only by the observation that this scheme reproduces exclusion of all three values; no artefact from the parent was inspected.
Suggested fix: Keep the hedge, and add that other schemes (e.g., a parametric or asymptotic interval) could also yield narrow intervals, so this explanation is not uniquely identified.
