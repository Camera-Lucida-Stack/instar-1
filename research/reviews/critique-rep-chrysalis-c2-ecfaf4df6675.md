Every number in the paper matches specification.json. However, the verdict 'replicated' is stronger than the evidence supports. The paper's own spread-to-interval ratio for beta (0.98) shows that specification does not dominate sampling uncertainty for beta, which contradicts the interpretive core of C2. The quoted allocation-exponent range matches the parent's 0.35 only because it includes a subset the paper itself calls poorly identified. The 'close to the original' part of C2 is endorsed without noting that Hoffmann's alpha lies outside the C>=1e19 interval. The main argument for specification effects is a single non-overlap comparison between nested samples, which is not a valid test of a difference. No limitations are stated, and the digitisation-noise check was not extended to the compute cutoffs, where threshold reassignment matters. The outcome should be downgraded to partial replication, with C2's numeric and interpretive components assessed separately.

**major** (outcome): The outcome 'replicated' conflicts with the paper's own evidence. Among well-identified cutoffs (n>=100), the spread of beta is 0.98 times the width of its 90% interval on all runs (specification.json ratio_well.beta = 0.9807). So for beta, specification does not move the estimate by more than sampling uncertainty, and the claim's 'specification-dominated' language is not supported for that parameter. Only the allocation exponent (ratio 1.70) and one hand-picked pairwise comparison support the claim. Claim C2 is compound: its numeric reproduction holds, while its interpretive part ('specification-dominated', 'dramatically understate') is only partly supported.
Suggested fix: Change the outcome to partially replicated, or split C2 into its parts. Report that the numbers at C>=1e19 and the allocation-exponent sensitivity replicate, and that specification dominance for beta does not.

**major** (evidence (allocation exponent range)): The range '[0.36, 0.56] across cutoffs, consistent with the claimed 0.35 to 0.56' takes its lower end from the C>=3e20 cutoff (n=63). The paper itself labels that cutoff poorly identified and leaves it out of the ratio calculation. Over well-identified cutoffs the range is [0.376, 0.565] (spread_well.a_opt). The low end of the parent's range therefore rests on an unreliable fit, and the paper treats cutoffs inconsistently: it includes the n=63 fit for the range but excludes it for the ratio. In addition, the minimum 0.3557 rounds to 0.36, not 0.35.
Suggested fix: Report the range over well-identified cutoffs, 0.38 to 0.56. State that the parent's 0.35 depends on a poorly identified subset.

**major** (evidence (C>=1e19 'matching the claim')): C2 says the C>=1e19 fit is 'close to the original'. The paper confirms the point estimates but never tests closeness. In specification.json the 1e19 alpha interval is [0.354, 0.396], which excludes Hoffmann's alpha=0.34. The original is therefore not recovered within sampling error for alpha (beta and E are inside their intervals). Endorsing 'matching the claim' without noting this overstates the near-recovery.
Suggested fix: Report which of Hoffmann's values (alpha, beta, E) fall inside the C>=1e19 90% intervals, and qualify the 'close to the original' part of C2.

**major** (evidence (non-overlap argument)): The case that specification exceeds sampling uncertainty rests on non-overlapping 90% intervals between the full data and the C>=1e19 subset. This comparison has three weaknesses. (i) The two samples are nested, not independent: the 192 runs are a subset of the 245. (ii) Interval non-overlap is not a test of a difference. (iii) It is the single most extreme pair, chosen after the fact; adjacent cutoffs such as 3e18 vs 1e19, or 1e19 vs 1e20, may well overlap.
Suggested fix: Bootstrap the difference in beta and in a between specifications by resampling the full data and applying both cutoffs to each resample. Alternatively, report overlap for all cutoff pairs rather than one.

**major** (limitations (absent)): The paper states no limitations. The data are digitised from an SVG figure. The extraction-noise check in quality.json was run only on the trimmed full dataset, not on the compute cutoffs. Cutoff membership depends directly on reconstructed C, and points near a threshold could switch sides under digitisation error, so the cutoff fits may be more fragile than reported. A second unstated issue is interpretive: the spread across cutoffs may reflect misspecification of the functional form, since different compute regions estimate different targets, rather than 'uncertainty' in a single parameter.
Suggested fix: Run the extraction-noise perturbation at each cutoff, including reassignment of points across thresholds. State the digitisation and misspecification caveats explicitly.

**minor** (code (specification.py boot)): Each bootstrap fit starts from five jittered copies of the full-sample optimum, not from the grid. This can bias resamples toward the full-sample basin and narrow the intervals, especially for poorly identified cutoffs where median_max_parameter_move exceeds 1. The paper's wording 'refitted from the full grid' could be read as applying to the bootstrap as well. Non-converged resamples (up to 4 per cutoff) are kept in the percentiles without comment.
Suggested fix: Say explicitly that the bootstrap uses local multistart. Report sensitivity to excluding non-converged resamples, or use the coarse grid for each resample.

**minor** (evidence (bootstrap size)): Each 90% percentile interval comes from 200 resamples, so each tail rests on about 10 draws. The Monte Carlo error on the interval endpoints is non-trivial, and the non-overlap and ratio conclusions depend on those endpoints.
Suggested fix: Increase the number of resamples (for example to 1000), or report Monte Carlo error on the endpoints.

**minor** (evidence (independent implementation)): The 'agreement within 8e-8' covers only alpha, beta, E and a; A and B are not compared. With bounds up to 1e8 or 1e9 on A and B, the agreement on these weakly identified scale parameters is unverified.
Suggested fix: Include A and B in the comparison, or limit the agreement statement to the listed parameters.

**minor** (evidence (ratio definition)): The spread ratio divides by the width of the full-data interval only. The interval widths vary a lot by cutoff; at 1e20 the beta interval is about twice as wide as on the full data. Dividing by the per-cutoff or average width would give smaller ratios, so the denominator choice is not neutral.
Suggested fix: Report the ratio against per-cutoff interval widths as well, or justify the choice of denominator.
